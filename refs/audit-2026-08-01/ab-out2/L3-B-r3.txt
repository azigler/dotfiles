**Substantive — "low-stakes" depends on one thing you may not have checked.** Exponential vs fixed 5s is genuinely taste *if* the retry sits behind a background job. It stops being taste if the retry is inside a caller-facing request path with a timeout or SLA: fixed 5s has a predictable worst case, exponential's tail blows past a client deadline unless it's capped. So the questions worth a minute:

- Is there a **max backoff cap** and a **max attempt count**, and does attempts × cap fit under the caller's timeout?
- Is there **jitter**? Uncapped exponential without jitter re-synchronizes retries into thundering herds after an outage — which is the failure mode fixed-5s is usually chosen to avoid in the first place.

If both are handled, your judgment holds and the bead is just bookkeeping. If the retry is caller-facing and uncapped, that's the part I'd look at before moving on — and it'd be worth noting in the bead's Reversibility line, since a cap is a much cheaper change now than after callers start depending on the timing.
