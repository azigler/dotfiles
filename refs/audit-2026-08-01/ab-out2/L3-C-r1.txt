No, don't revisit it. Exponential is the conventional-correct default for retries, and "which backoff curve" is exactly the kind of fork that's cheaper to decide than to escalate. You read the stakes right.

Two things worth a follow-up, though — not because the choice was wrong, but because it's underspecified in a way that fixed-5s wasn't:

**Check for jitter.** Fixed 5s and un-jittered exponential share a failure mode: N clients that fail at the same moment retry at the same moment, forever. Exponential without jitter doesn't spread retries, it just spreads them *further apart* while keeping them synchronized. If the retry loop you wrote doesn't randomize the delay, that's the one real bug hiding in this decision. Full jitter (`sleep = random(0, min(cap, base * 2^n))`) is the usual fix.

**Check for a cap and a total deadline.** Doubling gets to multi-minute waits in about seven attempts. If anything upstream has a timeout — or a user is waiting — bound total elapsed time rather than attempt count, so the retry budget can't outlive the thing that cares about the result.

Also worth knowing: if the service returns `Retry-After` or a rate-limit reset timestamp, honor that over your own schedule regardless of which curve you picked. That overrides the fork entirely.
